# Analyzing Systemic Governance Loops

**Context:** This applies when examining complex, automated, or human-driven procedures where a process repeats, stalls, or continues indefinitely without obvious external cause.

## Problem
The problem is mistaking symptoms (e.g., persistent repetition, indefinite continuation, timeout errors) for the root cause, leading to superficial fixes that fail because they do not address the underlying mechanical rules governing the system's flow and termination.

## Solution
Implement a diagnostic shift: move the focus away from analyzing observable actions or explicit belief states, toward interrogating the *governing mechanics*. Specifically, identify three critical structural dependencies: 1) Required points of external validation (closure/opening); 2) Explicit termination conditions (the decision boundary), and 3) The mechanism by which uncertainty (ambiguity) is interpreted as permission to continue processing.

## When to Use
When any repeatable process—whether code execution, procedural negotiation, or creative workflow—exhibits unexpected persistence, cycles back to previously resolved ambiguities, or fails without a clear, explicit decision point for termination, despite the appearance of having sufficient logical checks in place (e.g., similar contexts requiring validation points, when a particular individual reports difficulty stopping a recurring task).
